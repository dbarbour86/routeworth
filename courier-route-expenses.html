The Big Three Route Costs

Most courier route decisions start with three numbers: fuel, maintenance, and time. If those are wrong, the profit estimate will be wrong too.

FuelGas price, MPG, and total miles
MaintenanceTires, oil, brakes, repairs, wear
TimeDriving, waiting, pickup, drop-off

Fuel Cost

Fuel cost depends on total miles, your vehicle MPG, and the current gas price. For route decisions, use the price you actually pay locally and your real-world MPG, not the best MPG your vehicle has ever shown.

Fuel cost (Total miles / MPG) x gas price

If a route includes return miles or unpaid driving after drop-off, include those miles. Fuel does not care whether the mile was paid or unpaid.

Maintenance Cost

Maintenance is easy to ignore because it usually does not come out of your pocket on the same day. But every route adds wear to tires, brakes, oil, suspension, and the vehicle itself.

RouteWorth defaults maintenance to $0.15 per mile. Some drivers may need a higher or lower number depending on vehicle age, repair history, tire cost, and how much stop-and-go driving the route involves.

Maintenance cost Total miles x maintenance cost per mile

Taxes and Mileage Deduction

Independent couriers usually need to think about taxes differently than employees. Mileage deductions can reduce taxable income, but they are not the same thing as cash profit. RouteWorth shows estimated mileage deduction value separately so drivers can see it without confusing it with take-home route profit.

This is also why good records matter. Track dates, business miles, route details, and expenses so tax time is not guesswork.

Deadhead Miles

Deadhead miles are unpaid miles you drive to reach pickup, get back home, or reposition after delivery. These miles can turn a good-looking route into a borderline route because they add fuel and maintenance cost without adding payout.

When comparing routes, count the route the way you actually have to drive it, not only the miles between pickup and drop-off.

Waiting Time and Facility Delays

Medical courier work can include waiting at labs, pharmacies, clinics, hospitals, or long-term care facilities. That time affects your effective hourly rate. A route that pays well per mile can still be weak if it traps you in unpaid waiting time.

Other Costs Drivers Forget

  • Tolls, parking, and access fees.
  • Commercial insurance or rideshare/delivery coverage differences.
  • Phone plan, chargers, mounts, bags, coolers, or route supplies.
  • Cleaning, PPE, or delivery-specific equipment.
  • Vehicle depreciation from high-mileage work.
